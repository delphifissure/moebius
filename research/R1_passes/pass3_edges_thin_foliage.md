# Pass 3 — Depth edges, soft boundaries, thin structures, foliage and matting (agent report, lightly trimmed)

Access note from the pass: arXiv/CVF/Springer/ScienceDirect blocked; verified from abstracts, GitHub code/READMEs, and Microsoft-hosted PDFs; body details not readable are marked (unverified).

## 1. Depth-boundary sharpening / refinement
- SharpNet, Ramamonjisoa & Lepetit, ICCV-W 2019 (1905.08598): depth + normals + occluding contours with consensus losses; trained on synthetic PBRS then NYUv2 because real GT is unreliable at contours; evaluated with Canny on depth (NYUv2-OC). A training recipe; evidence that edge blur is a label-noise artefact, not intrinsic.
- Displacement Fields, Ramamonjisoa, Du & Lepetit, CVPR 2020 (2002.12730): a small net predicts a 2-D displacement field that RE-SAMPLES the blurred depth so the ramp collapses onto one side; trainable on synthetic data only. Operative idea: resample, don't smooth — each ramp pixel copies a plateau depth, never keeps the intermediate value. A weighted-median snap is the training-free equivalent.
- Boundary-aware losses: BAD loss (2110.05885); "The Devil is in the Edges" (2404.00373); Depth Pro second-stage gradient losses; Koch et al. iBims-1 depth boundary error (ECCV-W 2018, 1805.01328; exact ε_acc/ε_comp unverified). Inference-irrelevant; Koch's finding that CNN depth is systematically over-smoothed at edges is the root cause.
- Edge-guided upsampling / filters: joint bilateral upsampling (Kopf et al., SIGGRAPH 2007); guided filter (He, Sun & Tang, ECCV 2010 / TPAMI 2013; halos from its fixed regulariser); Fast Bilateral Solver (Barron & Poole, ECCV 2016; global, has a confidence input). All are means: they re-blur a ramp rather than snap it, and follow RGB edges (texture edges for foliage). If used, FBS with confidence 0 inside the ramp band; otherwise a median.
- The one training-free recipe with published constants — Shih et al., CVPR 2020 (config verified): MiDaS disparity min-subtracted and scaled to [0, 3]; discontinuity |Δdisparity| > 0.04 (≈ 1.3 % of range ≈ 3 grey levels of an 8-bit map, above quantisation); sparse bilateral weighted MEDIAN (σ_s 4 px, σ_r 0.5, windows 7,7,5,5,5, 5 iterations) with the discontinuity mask zeroing weights across the edge — the ramp-snapping step; small discontinuity components dropped (min_node_in_cc); edges dilated 10 then 5 px for inpainting context. Cheap (5 passes of ≤ 7 px); constants defensible: window ≈ 2× ramp width, threshold ≈ 3–10× LSB.

## 2. Depth Pro's boundary metric and what "sharp" means
Bochkovskii et al. 2024 (2410.02073; eval/boundary_metrics.py): an occluding contour exists between 4-neighbours iff d_j/d_i > t; F1 per t ∈ linspace(1.05, 1.25, 10), weights ∝ t. On matting datasets (hair, fur) recall of the mask boundary with mask = alpha > 0.1 after NMS. Consequences: (i) the ratio test is scale-free and the right discontinuity test for a normalised map; (ii) "sharp" is scored as the depth edge sitting at the alpha = 0.1 contour, the OUTERMOST extent of hair/foliage — a model that scores well assigns foreground depth to the entire silhouette including gaps. That is precisely the bristlecone failure: sharp-boundary models are trained to make porous silhouettes solid. Don't expect a better depth model to fix porosity; it must be recovered from the image.

## 3. Flying / mixed pixels
Sabov & Krüger, SCCG 2008: ToF pixels straddling an edge land at intermediate depth; corrected by fitting linear segments along scan-lines and assigning each pixel to one. Colour-guided flying-pixel correction 2024 (2410.08084, internals unverified). Practitioner "depth discontinuity filter": discard pixels differing from neighbours by more than a threshold. The mono-depth ramp is the same phenomenon with a wider kernel: every ramp pixel is a flying pixel; sensor practice removes them, never keeps them as geometry. Verdict: the mesh tear is the right default; add the sensor-style test in the shader — a triangle whose normal is near-perpendicular to the view ray (a skirt) is a flying triangle regardless of any depth threshold: threshold-free, view-dependent, valid to 45°.

## 4. Soft / alpha layering at depth edges
- Zitnick et al., SIGGRAPH 2004 (verified from the PDF): two-layer representation; discontinuity = disparity jump > 4 px; within a 4-pixel neighbourhood of every discontinuity run Bayesian matting for foreground colour, alpha and depth = alpha-weighted average of nearby depths; the foreground part becomes a sparse "boundary layer" (~1/64 of the data), background stays in the main layer; matte dilated 1 px inward against cracks; triangles spanning a discontinuity erased in a separate pass; rendering blends layers normalised by alpha. Essentially our main mesh + plug design with an added alpha-matted foreground strip.
- Hasinoff, Kang & Szeliski, "Boundary matting for view synthesis", CVIU 2006: occlusion boundaries as sub-pixel 3-D curves with alpha; fuzzy boundaries (hair, foliage) the stated limitation.
- Soft 3D, Penner & Zhang, SIGGRAPH Asia 2017: per-pixel depth DISTRIBUTION through reconstruction and rendering; soft visibility; needs multiple views. Single-image analogue: render the ramp as two hypotheses (near plateau with alpha from ramp position, far plateau behind).
- SLIDE, Jampani et al., ICCV 2021 (2109.01068): hard layering "cannot model thin hair-like structures"; a soft foreground-visibility map from disparity discontinuities, optionally × an alpha matte, splits the image into two layers rendered in one pass; users preferred it on hair, more so with matting (exact formula unverified). HairGuard 2026 (2601.03362): a "depth fixer" trained on matting datasets; αDepth 2026 (2606.00386): per-pixel soft-boundary decomposition for stereo conversion (both unverified beyond abstracts). Single-view MPI (Tucker & Snavely CVPR 2020): 32 RGBA planes absorb edge softness. Omnimatte (Lu et al. CVPR 2021): per-object RGBA layers, video only.
- Matting with depth for trimaps: depth-camera trimap generation with unknown-band width adapted to boundary fuzziness (IEEE 2011); depth segmentation → dilate/erode trimap (Springer 2016). Our discontinuity mask dilated by the ramp width is a free trimap; a matting net (or colour-line estimate) then gives alpha for hair/fur/foliage edges.

## 5. Foliage and porous structures
Volumetric methods use density/alpha per sample, not surfaces: NeRF recovers thin branches better than photogrammetry given coverage (Sapling-NeRF 2602.22731; Remote Sensing 2025); thin leaves still blur (NeDDF 2207.14455); 3DGS good at thin leaves and intricate silhouettes but dense foliage yields missing/spurious branches and needle-like Gaussians (GaussianPlant 2512.14087; LeafFit 2602.11577; Pixel-GS 2403.15530). Single-image tree work fills a crown ENVELOPE with leaf replicas (Tan et al., SIGGRAPH 2008; Tree-D Fusion, ECCV 2024): envelope + stochastic porosity, never per-gap depth. Verdict: a single image cannot give per-gap depth, but it can give alpha coverage inside the envelope; render the crown as a near layer with alpha and let the plug show through.

## 6. Thin structures
Depth Anything V2 (NeurIPS 2024) attributes thin-structure gains to pixel-perfect synthetic labels; Miangoleh et al. CVPR 2021: thin details appear only when inference resolution fits edge density to the receptive field (R0/R20 merging) — run depth at higher resolution on patches when thin features matter. CurveFusion (2107.05284): wire reconstruction from RGB-D via curve skeletons, fails when the wire is near a wall (depth resolution) — our 8-bit case. Physics: a structure of width w under blur width b > w never reaches its own depth (amplitude ≈ w/b), so it vanishes or sits mid-ramp and drags background. Verdict: detect ridges in disparity (top-hat / ridge filter, width ≤ 2b), restore their disparity by max-within-ridge, and tear both sides; a thin structure is exactly a pair of opposite-ownership occlusion edges within b pixels — a threshold-free detector given b.

## 7. Occlusion boundaries, ownership, layering without a membrane
Range-image segmentation distinguishes JUMP edges (discontinuities) from CREASE/roof edges (normal changes) by residuals of local plane fits, not gradients; gradient tests fire on creases and steep floors — relevant at 45° views. Hoiem, Efros & Hebert, IJCV 2011: occlusion boundaries + figure/ground from surface layout in a CRF. DOC (Wang & Yuille, 1511.06457), DOOBNet (ACCV 2018): boundary + orientation (left-hand rule ⇒ owner). P2ORM (ECCV 2020): each of the 8 neighbour pairs labelled occludes/occluded/none, used to refine depth at boundaries. DepthCut (Guerrero et al., SGP 2017, 1705.07844): fuses RGB, disparity and normals for depth edges, segments into flat-depth layers, constrains depth gradients to those edges. Sarkar et al. (1412.7007): occlusion-edge GT purely from Kinect depth. Verdict: with depth available, ownership is the sign of Δdepth; the useful import is edge SNAPPING — the RGB edge marks the owner's silhouette, so the ramp belongs to the far side; assign ramp pixels the far plateau unless the RGB edge says otherwise (cheap Displacement Fields). Layering by connected components of the discontinuity graph (Shih, DepthCut) replaces the membrane with explicit "who is in front of whom" per edge.

## What we should steal (prioritised by the pass)
1. Ratio discontinuity test + median snap (Depth Pro metric; Shih code): discontinuity iff d_far/d_near > t, t = 1.05–1.25 (or |Δdisp| ≥ 3–10 LSB on the 8-bit map); then 5 passes of a ≤ 7 px weighted median on disparity with weights zeroed across the mask. Removes the skirt without tearing thin slivers.
2. Zitnick's boundary layer: 4 px band at every discontinuity → matte (alpha, F colour, alpha-weighted depth) as a sparse foreground sprite layer; main mesh keeps the background; dilate the matte 1 px inward; erase spanning triangles.
3. Grazing-angle skirt kill in the shader (flying-pixel filter, threshold-free, view-dependent) as the safety net at 45°.
4. Ramp ownership = far side; snap to the RGB edge (Displacement Fields idea, DepthCut layering) instead of splitting the ramp at its midpoint.
5. Thin-ridge restore + double tear, using the pair-of-opposite-edges-within-b detector.
6. Alpha-coverage crown layer for porous silhouettes, alpha from colour matting inside the envelope, background from the plug.
7. Higher-resolution patch inference (Miangoleh) when thin features are detected — offline.

## How to tell a porous silhouette from a solid one, according to the literature
Forestry measures crown porosity from a silhouette alone without hand thresholds: gap fraction / crown transparency = gap pixels ÷ pixels inside the crown envelope (Comput. Electron. Agric.; hemispherical-photo gap fraction with Otsu-type binarisation, iForest 2014; "volume gap fraction", PMC 2023); Euler number of the binarised silhouette (components − holes) counts through-holes directly (Forestry 2018); DSO = D_box(silhouette) − D_box(outline), the fractal-dimension difference, ≈ 0 for a solid blob, large for porous crowns (same paper; ISPRS 2015); lacunarity for gap spacing. Recipe for image + depth: envelope from the depth discontinuity graph; inside it, classify pixels foliage vs background by colour (2-cluster / Otsu against the plug's rim colour) or by matting alpha with alpha < 0.1 as "gap"; porous when the Euler number is strongly negative AND gap fraction is non-trivial AND RGB shows edges where depth shows none (the blur radius sets the smallest gap depth can register, so any RGB hole below that size is diagnostic). Solid objects: Euler ≈ 1, gap fraction ≈ 0, RGB edges only at the depth edge. All "constants" are data-derived (Otsu, cluster) or inherited from published metrics (alpha 0.1; t ∈ [1.05, 1.25]; 4 px band).
